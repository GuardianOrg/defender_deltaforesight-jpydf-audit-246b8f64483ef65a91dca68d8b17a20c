# OPERATING AGREEMENT OF JPYdf Foundation DAO LLC

## NOTICE OF RESTRICTIONS ON DUTIES AND TRANSFERS

The rights of members in a decentralized autonomous organization may differ materially from the rights of members in other resident domestic or non-resident domestic limited liability companies. The DAO Act, underlying smart contracts, certificate of formation, and limited liability company agreement (i.e., the operating agreement), if applicable, of a decentralized autonomous organization may define, reduce, or eliminate fiduciary duties and may restrict the transfer of ownership interests, withdrawal or resignation from the decentralized autonomous organization, return of capital contributions and dissolution of the decentralized autonomous organization.

## Recital

This Operating Agreement ("the Agreement") of JPYdf Foundation DAO LLC, a non-profit limited liability company (the "Company") incorporated as per the laws of the Republic of the Marshall Islands pursuant to the Marshall Islands Limited Liability Company Act of 1996 (as amended, the "LLC Act"), the Marshall Islands Non-Profit Entities (Amendment) Act of 2021 (as amended, the "Non-Profit Act"), the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act (as amended, the "BCA") and the Decentralized Autonomous Organization Act, 2022 (as amended, the "DAO Act"), by and among the Company, the Members listed in Exhibit A (individually "Member" and collectively "Members"), and the holders of those Members, each holder executing this Agreement in respect of its obligations under it (Article III.2), causing the filing of the certificate of formation of the Company (the "Certificate of Formation") with the Marshall Islands Registrar of Corporations shall be effective as of the date of the filing of the Certificate of Formation ("the Effective Date"). Capitalized terms have the meanings given to them in the relevant law or expressly in this Agreement.

WHEREAS, in pursuit of compliance with a fit-for-purpose legal regime that seeks to protect members and support innovation, the Initial Members approved the formation of the Company as a Marshall Islands non-profit decentralized autonomous organization limited liability company.

NOW, THEREFORE, for and in consideration of mutual covenants contained and intending to be legally bound hereby, the parties agree as follows:

## ARTICLE I: ORGANIZATION

I.1 Organization. The Members, by executing this Agreement, hereby agree to organize as a Marshall Islands non-profit, member-managed decentralized autonomous organization limited liability company ("DAO LLC") pursuant to the provisions of the DAO Act, the LLC Act, the Non-Profit Act and the BCA upon the terms set forth in this Agreement.

I.2 Decentralized Autonomous Organization. The Company is a decentralized autonomous organization within the meaning of §102(c) and §104(1) and (3) of the DAO Act, whereby the notice of restrictions on duties and transfers appears conspicuously at the beginning of this Agreement.

I.3 Registered Office; Registered Agent. The registered office of the Company in the Marshall Islands shall be the initial registered office designated in the Certificate of Formation or such other office (which need not be a place of business of the Company) as provided by law. The registered agent of the Company in the Marshall Islands shall be the initial registered agent designated in the Certificate of Formation.

I.4 Principal Office; Official Online Venue. The Principal Office of the Company shall be at the principal office designated in the Certificate of Formation or at such other location as the Members may designate from time to time, which need not be in the Marshall Islands. The Company shall maintain the current Principal Office in its records under Article VI and make any filing required by applicable law. The Members may designate, change, or discontinue an Official Online Venue through Company Governance. The Company shall maintain any current designation in its records and make it publicly available to the extent required by applicable law.

I.5 Term. The Company shall commence upon the date of the filing of the Certificate of Formation and shall continue in existence until dissolved pursuant to this Agreement.

I.6 Continuing Compliance. The Company shall at all times maintain a registered agent in the Marshall Islands and a representative agent, in each case as required by applicable law, shall complete each annual registration renewal by its due date, and shall take reasonable steps to remain in good standing with the Marshall Islands DAO registry. The Company shall maintain a compliance calendar for these deadlines. If the office of representative agent, or the office of any other person on whose control the Company relies to satisfy its statutory requirements, becomes vacant, the Company shall fill the vacancy without undue delay and in any event before any period after which continued vacancy would cause dissolution under applicable law, and shall keep a standing succession plan for that purpose. The Company shall confirm from time to time the continuing good standing of the holder of each Member address (Article III.2). The Company shall include in its Certificate of Formation and any other required filing each publicly available smart-contract identifier required by applicable law, keep those identifiers current, and maintain the corresponding filing and implementation records under Article VI. Inclusion of an identifier in a filing or Company record does not create authority beyond Articles V.2 and V.4 or bring within the Company any Protocol arrangement excluded under Articles II.2 and V.3. This Article does not enlarge the authority of any Member or agent beyond the authorizations set out in Articles III.3 and V, and the discharge of these functions does not relieve the Company of this continuing obligation.

## ARTICLE II: PURPOSES AND POWERS

II.1 Nonprofit Character. The Company shall be a non-profit entity within the meaning of the Non-Profit Act and may exercise the powers and privileges conferred upon limited liability companies by the laws of the Marshall Islands only in furtherance of and subject to its company purpose.

II.2 Purposes of the Company. The Company is organized exclusively for charitable, educational, scientific, and other types of "good works" purposes and not for profit. Specifically, the Company shall act as a long-term steward and an administrative layer supporting the JPY DeFi Protocol and its governance. For purposes of this Agreement, the "Protocol" means the over-collateralized, yen-denominated CDP stablecoin smart contract system deployed on public blockchains and publicly identified as the JPY DeFi Protocol, together with any successor or upgraded deployment of that system however adopted. "Protocol Governance" means the then-effective governance process through which changes to the Protocol or exercises of authority over it are proposed, deliberated, approved, executed, and recorded. For the avoidance of doubt, Protocol Governance is separate from Company Governance. A decision or approval by the Members under Company Governance, or an action by the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet, does not, by itself, constitute a decision, approval, or execution under Protocol Governance.

The Company's purposes and permitted activities include:

1. holding and managing intellectual property, trademarks, domains, and documentation related to the Protocol and ecosystem;
2. entering into and administering agreements with contributors, service providers, and grant recipients related to the Protocol and ecosystem;
3. where a matter approved under Protocol Governance requires a company-side off-chain act, such as contract execution, recordkeeping, confirmation, notice, filing, or other administrative action, recording, verifying, and carrying out that company-side off-chain act;
4. managing Foundation Operating Assets for the Company's own non-profit operations, as assets separate from the Protocol Treasury, user assets, and other Protocol assets; and
5. acting as a legal and administrative interface and liability backstop for Protocol Claims.

The Company is not organized for, and no provision of this Agreement shall be construed as authorizing, any of the following:

1. acting or holding itself out as the issuer of JPYdf;
2. issuing or minting JPYdf;
3. undertaking, guaranteeing, or assuming any obligation to redeem or repurchase JPYdf or maintain its value;
4. acting as a counterparty to any user in connection with any borrowing, repayment, opening, adjustment, or closing of a Trove, Stability Pool deposit or withdrawal, redemption, liquidation, or other user transaction under the Protocol;
5. entering into, intermediating, accepting, rejecting, approving, or settling any such user transaction with any user as an act of the Company;
6. taking custody of, holding for or on behalf of a user, acquiring legal or beneficial title to, transferring, or exercising discretionary control over any user collateral, assets deposited into a Stability Pool, or assets recorded in or attributable to a Trove;
7. taking custody of, holding for or on behalf of a user, acquiring legal or beneficial title to, transferring, or exercising discretionary control over assets moving through any redemption or liquidation process or any other user asset;
8. holding any private key, signing authority, withdrawal authority, or other disposition right with respect to any such asset;
9. holding any signing key with respect to the Protocol's smart contracts, governance process, Protocol Treasury, risk controls, emergency controls, bridge controls, parameter setting, asset allocation, or any similar function of the Protocol, or serving in any position authorized to decide, approve, direct, sign, execute, or modify any matter concerning any such function, including as a signer, role holder, administrator, operator, controller, or any equivalent position;
10. directly, or through any signer, agent, wallet, contract, or other arrangement acting for or on behalf of the Company, deciding, approving, re-approving, or directing any matter under Protocol Governance;
11. directly, or through any signer, agent, wallet, contract, or other arrangement acting for or on behalf of the Company, signing or submitting any transaction that executes a Protocol Governance decision on-chain, implementing on-chain any change or upgrade to a Protocol smart contract, transferring any asset from the Protocol Treasury or any other Protocol asset, or appointing or removing any Protocol role holder;
12. acting in place of Protocol Governance, operating or pausing the Protocol, or otherwise exercising substantive control over it; or
13. owning, managing, or disposing of the Protocol Treasury as the Company's own property, balance-sheet asset, or freely disposable asset.

II.3 Authority of the Company. The Company shall have and exercise all of the powers and authority conferred by Marshall Islands law on an entity recognized as a decentralized autonomous organization limited liability company with non-profit status, and insofar as this entity form is recognized by foreign laws as a legal person separate and distinct from its Members, then the Company shall also have all of the powers and authority as conferred by those foreign laws.

II.4 No Private Inurement. No part of its assets, income, or earnings shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to, any person or individual, including a Member or non-Member or any private individual of the Company, during the life or upon dissolution of the Company, except:

1. In accordance with the advancement of the Company's purpose;
2. So that reasonable compensation may be paid for services rendered to or for the Company advancing its purpose; and
3. For reimbursement of actual and reasonable expenses incurred on behalf of the Company for advancing its purpose where the Company approved the amount, nature, and recipient in advance or, in the case of reasonable pre-formation expenses, ratified them after formation.

II.5 Limitation of Non-Profit Activities. In accordance with the Non-Profit Act, the Company maintains non-profit status if it exclusively engages in raising or disbursing funds for charitable, religious, scientific, educational, social, fraternal, or any other types of "good works" if:

1. No part of the net earnings inures to the benefit of any person or individual;
2. Its activities do not exclusively involve disseminating propaganda or otherwise attempting to influence legislation; and
3. It does not participate in, intervene in, or disseminate statements on behalf of or in opposition to any candidate running for public office.

The Company shall never be operated for the primary purpose of carrying on a trade or business for profit.

II.6 Smart-Contract Execution; No Control or Attribution from Permitted Activities.

- (a) Any minting of JPYdf is executed solely by the Protocol's smart contracts under the then-effective code and rules, and not by the Company. Each user transaction described in Article II.2 is initiated by a user or other Protocol participant and executed by the Protocol's smart contracts under the then-effective code and rules, without approval by the Company.
- (b) The Company's performance of any activity relating to the Protocol and ecosystem that is permitted under Article II.2 does not, by itself, cause the JPY DeFi Protocol, any of its smart contracts, or any implementation of it to be treated as the Company. Nor does such performance, by itself, constitute a basis for treating the Company as owning, operating, or controlling the Protocol or cause any user asset or Protocol asset to become an asset of the Company.
- (c) The Company's authority under this Agreement is limited to the company-side acts expressly enumerated in Article II.2 and elsewhere in this Agreement and shall not be expanded by implication or otherwise. Nothing in this Article expands the authority of the Company or any signer or agent acting for or on behalf of the Company, or overrides any restriction in Articles II.2 or V.2.

II.7 Future Governance Mechanisms; Governance-Token Issuance. If and only if the Members later determine through Company Governance that it is necessary, the Company may, by amendment of this Agreement under Article XI and in accordance with applicable law, adopt future tokenholder governance, algorithmic governance, one or more Councils, delegates, governance-token issuance, or any other governance mechanism. Any governance token shall remain governance-only, confer no ownership or economic rights, and comply with the Company's nonprofit character.

## ARTICLE III: MEMBERS

III.1 Member Eligibility. A Membership Interest, which in the case of a non-profit DAO means a voting or governance right (not an ownership or economic right), is held through, and attributed to, a wallet address (a Digital Ledger identifier, also referred to in this Agreement as a Distributed Ledger identifier). Membership shall be limited to the wallet addresses that are set forth in "Exhibit A" attached hereto, each of which is a Member. No token, including JPYdf, confers any Membership Interest under this Agreement.

III.2 Initial Members; Holders. The Initial Members are the wallet addresses reflected in "Exhibit A" attached hereto. The person that holds or controls a Member address (its "holder") is not, by reason of that holding or control, a Member and holds no Membership Interest of its own. Each holder is responsible for the performance of that Member's obligations under this Agreement, shall pass the restricted-person screening applicable under Articles III and IX, and shall complete such KYC / KYB (including of its ultimate beneficial owners) as required by applicable law and the Company's then-current compliance procedures. An event affecting a holder is attributed to its Member address where this Agreement so provides (Article VIII.1). No holder may hold or control more than one Member address (one holder, one address).

III.3 Nominee Member. The Company may appoint one nominee member of record (the "nominee member") to act as the Company's agent for the public-facing registration and compliance functions set out in this Article. The nominee member is a member of record in name only, for administrative and compliance purposes. It holds no Membership Interest, has no voting, governance, economic, or distribution right, is not counted among the Members for any purpose (including the number of Members, quorum, and any voting or approval threshold under this Agreement), and is not one of the wallet addresses set forth in "Exhibit A". The nominee member holds no signer address of, and is not a signer of, the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet.

Acting under, and only within the scope of, a Company Governance Approval that expressly authorizes it in accordance with Article V.4, the nominee member: (a) prepares and, where applicable, signs the Company's registration, formation, dissolution, and other documents required by applicable law and delivers them to the Registered Agent for filing with the Registrar or other competent authority; (b) completes any wallet screening and further KYC / KYB required by applicable law, the Registered Agent, or another competent authority; (c) signs and delivers the Company's beneficial owner information report (BOIR) to the Registered Agent for filing; (d) prepares the Company's annual renewal and other annual filings, with input from the Members, and delivers the required documents to the Registered Agent; and (e) serves as the Company's Representative Agent, maintains the contact information required by applicable law with the Registered Agent and the government of the Republic of the Marshall Islands, and receives correspondence and service of process forwarded by the Registered Agent. The authority of the nominee member is limited to these administrative and compliance functions, and it derives no other authority from this Agreement.

The Members appoint, and may replace, the nominee member through Company Governance. The identity, engagement agreement, and particulars of the nominee member are maintained in the Company's records under Article VI, and a successor nominee member is appointed in the same manner.

III.4 Classes of Membership. The Company has one class of Members holding Membership Interests, being the wallet addresses set forth in "Exhibit A". Separately, if appointed, the Company may have one nominee member under Article III.3, which holds no Membership Interest and is not a Member for the purposes of this Agreement. In this Agreement, "Member" and "Members" refer only to the wallet addresses holding a Membership Interest under this Article, and do not include the nominee member unless the nominee member is expressly named.

III.5 Voting Weight; Member Decisions. Each Member has one (1) vote. Each Member holds an equal Membership Interest representing twenty percent (20%) of the governance and voting rights of the Company; no Member holds, and no person shall be admitted or permitted to come to hold, twenty-five percent (25%) or more of the governance rights, voting rights, or managerial control of the Company except through an amendment adopted under Article XI.3. Unless a higher threshold is required by this Agreement, a decision of the Members is taken by an affirmative vote of at least three of the five Members (a 3-of-5 majority), in accordance with Company Governance; no single party can carry a decision alone. A voluntary dissolution under Article X.1(a) and any amendment that would defeat a Protected Provision under Article XI.3 require an affirmative vote of at least four of the five Members (4-of-5). The numeric formulae in this Agreement expressed as three of the five Members (3-of-5) and four of the five Members (4-of-5) apply while the Company has five Members; if the Company has a different number of Members, the corresponding at-least-a-majority and at-least-four-fifths fractions (in each case rounded up) of all Members then admitted apply wherever those formulae appear in this Agreement.

For purposes of this Agreement, "Company Governance" means the procedures established by this Agreement, together with any governance framework or policy duly adopted under and subject to this Agreement, through which the Members propose, consider, approve or reject, and record decisions concerning matters within the Company's authority. Such matters include Foundation affairs and company-side acts attached to Protocol Governance decisions, but exclude the underlying Protocol decision and any exercise of a key, role, authority, or function described in Article II.2. Company Governance is separate from, and does not constitute or replace, Protocol Governance. Execution by the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet or an expressly authorized agent does not itself constitute a decision or approval under Company Governance, except solely as a method for recording or executing Member votes or consents where permitted under this Article.

A vote or consent of a Member may be given at a meeting, by written or electronic consent (including email), by cryptographic signature from the Member address or from another wallet designated for that purpose through Company Governance and maintained in the Company's records under Article VI, or, where the Members so resolve, by an action of the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet solely as the recording or execution method for votes or consents that satisfy the applicable threshold under this Article (Article V.2). A proposal shall provide information reasonably sufficient for the Members to determine the proposed action, authority, material financial or legal effect, conflicts, and intended execution. A governance framework or policy adopted through Company Governance may govern notice and objection periods, urgent-compliance procedures, voting mechanics, and records, but may not reduce or replace any threshold, denominator, recusal requirement, or authority restriction in this Agreement. Any urgent-compliance procedure shall be limited to preserving the Company's legal status or preventing irreversible harm, shall state the reason for its use in the decision record, and shall not authorize a matter reserved to a higher threshold.

A Member whose holder is the counterparty to, or an affiliate of a counterparty to, a related-party matter shall recuse itself from the vote on that matter. A Member that recuses itself, abstains, or fails to vote is not counted toward the affirmative vote. For an ordinary matter, quorum requires participation by at least the number of Members whose affirmative votes would be required to approve that matter. For a proposal under Article X.1(a) or a matter under Article XI.3, quorum requires participation by at least four of the five Members and the denominator is always all Members then admitted. If recusal, abstention, or failure to vote or respond makes the applicable affirmative threshold unattainable, the matter is not adopted and the status quo is preserved. A renewed proposal requires new or supplemented supporting records and the same applicable threshold. The Company shall retain the proposal, participation and vote or consent record, recusals, satisfaction of any applicable notice or objection period, approval, and evidence of execution under Article VI.

The proposal, review, and record procedure for Foundation affairs is set out in Company Governance; company-side acts attached to Protocol Governance decisions are further conditioned by Article V.4.

III.6 Restrictions on Transfer of Membership Interests. A Membership Interest is personal to the Member to whom it is issued and may not be sold, assigned, transferred, pledged, or otherwise disposed of, in whole or in part, whether voluntarily, involuntarily, or by operation of law, except with the prior approval of the Company through Company Governance and subject to the transferee satisfying the eligibility, Restricted Person, and KYC/KYB requirements of Articles III and IX. A change of the holder of a Member address, being a transfer of the holding or control of that address, is deemed a transfer of the Membership Interest for the purposes of this Article. Any purported transfer in contravention of this Article is void and of no effect and shall confer no rights on the purported transferee. A Membership Interest carries no ownership or economic right (Article III.1), and nothing in this Article creates any such right.

III.7 Interested Persons; Concentration Cap; Related-Party Recusal. For purposes of this Agreement, an "Interested Person" means, with respect to a particular Company matter, any person that is a party or counterparty to that matter, has a material interest in that matter, exercises voting, approval, or other decision-making authority in relation to that matter, or is an affiliate of any such person. For this purpose, an "affiliate" of a person means any other person that directly or indirectly controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with that person, and "control" means the power to direct the management or policies of a person, whether through the ownership of voting rights, by contract, or otherwise. No person, together with its affiliates, may, directly or indirectly, hold or control more than one (1) Member address, more than twenty percent (20%) of the governance and voting rights of the Company, or more than one signer address of the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet, except through an amendment adopted under Article XI.3. No person may hold any right to appoint, remove, instruct, or veto any other Member or its holder, except for voting and approval rights held by Members generally under this Agreement. A Member whose holder is an Interested Person with respect to a related-party matter shall recuse itself as provided in Article III.5, and related-party transactions and payments shall be governed by Article VII.4. The independence of the Members' holders and the control-domain separation of the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet shall be evidenced and maintained in the Company's records under Article VI. Any purported admission, transfer, listing, or grant of rights in contravention of this Article is void and of no effect. Status as an Interested Person does not, by itself, reduce, suspend, or condition any Member's vote except as expressly provided in this Agreement. This Article is a Protected Provision and shall not be amended or defeated other than through the heightened process of Article XI.3.

## ARTICLE IV: RIGHTS AND DUTIES OF MEMBERS

IV.1 Management Rights. All Members who have not Dissociated (as defined in Article VIII) shall be entitled to vote on any matter submitted to a vote of the Members as provided in Article V.1.

IV.2 Liability of Members and Nominee Member. Except as otherwise provided by applicable law, the debts, obligations, and liabilities of the Company, whether arising in contract, tort, or otherwise, shall be solely the debts, obligations, and liabilities of the Company, and no Member or nominee member shall be obligated personally for any such debt, obligation, or liability solely by reason of being a Member or nominee member. The failure of the Company to observe any formality or requirement relating to the exercise of its powers or the management of its business or affairs under this Agreement shall not, standing alone, be grounds for imposing personal liability on any Member or nominee member for any debt, obligation, or liability of the Company.

IV.3 No Fiduciary Duties Unless Expressly Approved. In accordance with §109 of the DAO Act, no Member shall have any fiduciary duty to the Company or any Member unless otherwise provided for in this Agreement or any governance rules approved in accordance with this Agreement.

IV.4 Good Faith and Fair Dealing. In accordance with §109 of the DAO Act, Members are subject to the implied contractual covenants of good faith and fair dealing in respect of their interactions with the Company or any Member.

IV.5 Miscellaneous Obligations. Each Member shall be duty-bound to act consistently and in compliance with each of the provisions of this Agreement and with all policies, rules, and decisions of the Company adopted in accordance with this Agreement at all times.

IV.6 Duties of Authorized Signers and Agents. Because §109 of the DAO Act does not impose default fiduciary duties, the following duties are expressly established by this Agreement. Each person authorized to sign or act on behalf of the Company (including each signer of the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet) shall: (a) exercise reasonable care in performing their authorized ministerial functions; (b) act in good faith and in furtherance of the Company's non-profit purpose; and (c) maintain and preserve records of their decisions and actions as required by Article VI. The duties in this Article are contractual undertakings established by this Agreement pursuant to §109 of the DAO Act and do not revive or create fiduciary duties beyond its terms.

IV.7 No Implied Assumption of Company Debt. Neither this Agreement nor any governance rule adopted pursuant to it constitutes, or shall be construed as, an agreement by any Member under section 20(2) of the LLC Act to be obligated personally for any debt, obligation, or liability of the Company. A Member may assume such personal liability only in a separate written agreement that expressly states that the Member is assuming personal liability and the extent of that liability, and that is expressly executed by that Member. Notwithstanding Article XI, no amendment, waiver, or other modification of this Article IV.7 that would impose or permit personal liability on a Member is effective with respect to that Member unless the requirements of the preceding sentence are satisfied. Nothing in Articles IV.2 or IV.7 limits any liability arising under applicable law on a basis independent of a person's status as a Member, including liability for that person's own acts or omissions.

IV.8 Independence of Member Voting. No Member shall exercise its vote or any other governance right under this Agreement under the direction or instruction of any other Member, any holder of another Member address, any service provider, grant counterparty, or other external person. This Article does not prevent a Member from exercising its own vote under this Agreement in its own capacity and judgment, subject to the recusal requirements for related-party matters under Articles III.5 and III.7.

## ARTICLE V: MANAGEMENT

V.1 Member-Managed. The Company shall be member-managed, and decisions shall be voted on through Company Governance. No Manager or Managing Member is appointed under this Agreement, and the Company has no manager within the meaning of the LLC Act. Each Member has one vote and, unless a higher threshold is required by this Agreement, a decision requires an affirmative vote of at least three of the five Members (3-of-5) (Article III.5).

V.2 Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet. The Company shall maintain a multi-signature wallet for executing approved Foundation affairs (the "Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet"). The Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet is an execution mechanism only and does not itself constitute or replace Member approval under Article III.5. Its public identifier, signer set, execution threshold, and any later change shall be separately approved through Company Governance and maintained in a written designation and corresponding implementation record under Article VI rather than incorporated into this Agreement. The initial designation may be made by a separate written consent of the Initial Members effective upon formation. Each signer shall accept the role in writing, and no designation, acceptance, or wallet configuration creates authority beyond Article V.4.

Membership and signer status are distinct: a Member is not required to be a signer, a signer is not required to be a Member, and neither status creates the other. The execution threshold shall require more than one independent signer, shall not permit one signer to execute alone or hold a permanent unilateral veto, and, except during a protective freeze permitted by this Agreement, shall remain attainable by the independent remaining signers following the temporary unavailability of one signer. The execution threshold records execution only and does not reduce or replace the Member-approval threshold in Article III.5. Foundation affairs means the Company's own corporate matters, namely: entering into and performing the Company's contracts; managing the Foundation Operating Assets (Article VII.1); holding and administering Protocol-related intellectual property, trademarks, domains, and documentation; making approved grant and service payments; paying and administering counsel, insurance, registration, annual-filing, and beneficial-ownership-reporting matters; handling claims within Article XIII; and keeping records under Article VI.

The Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet shall be used solely for Foundation affairs. It shall not be designated, configured, or used to hold or exercise any key, role, authority, or function prohibited to the Company under Article II.2, or to take any action prohibited to the Company under that Article. A signer or expressly authorized agent is subject to the same restriction when acting as a signer of the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet, as an agent of the Company, or otherwise for or on behalf of the Company. This restriction does not prevent that person from acting separately in a Protocol capacity under Article V.3. Company-side actions executed through the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet, a signer, or an expressly authorized agent shall comply with Article V.4. A signer of the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet shall refuse or withhold its signature for any transaction that does not match an approved decision or mandate, and such refusal does not constitute a breach of this Agreement.

Each signer shall promptly report suspected loss, compromise, shared control, sanctions exposure, loss of good standing, or another fact affecting eligibility. Pending verification, the Company may take only protective and reversible steps and shall not treat possession of another person's private key as an acceptable replacement. A signer change requires Company Governance approval; following an approved change, the remaining signers shall rotate or reconstitute the wallet without undue delay. The Company shall retain records reasonably sufficient to evidence each signer's acceptance, independence, control domain, capacity, source of authority, and each designation, implementation, change, and verification. Identity materials, signing credentials, and other secrets shall remain non-public and shall not form part of this Agreement.

V.3 Protocol Signer Separation. Multi-signature and other signing or execution arrangements governed by the then-effective Protocol Governance, together with their addresses, signers, roles, thresholds, decisions, transactions, and records, are outside this Agreement and "Exhibit A". Being a Member or a signer of the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet does not create any Protocol role or authority. Conversely, a Protocol address, signer status, role, threshold, decision, transaction, or record does not create Membership, status as a signer of the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet, agency, or authority to bind the Company. A person may separately serve in a Foundation capacity and a Protocol capacity only if each capacity uses a separate wallet address and associated signing credentials, separate records, a separately stated reason for each signature or execution, and a separately identified source of authority. Authority for a Protocol signature or execution derives solely from the then-effective Protocol Governance and is evidenced by the applicable decision and execution records. Company-side authority is governed by Article V.4. No status, act, or record in either capacity creates, evidences, expands, or is attributed as status or authority in the other.

V.4 Authority to Bind the Company. For purposes of this Agreement, a "Company Governance Approval" means a decision concerning an action within the Company's authority that the Members have approved under Article III.5 and recorded under Company Governance. Only the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet acting through its signers under a Company Governance Approval, or an agent expressly authorized by that approval, may execute an action that binds the Company, and only within the scope of that approval. Holding a key, serving or being listed as a wallet signer or Member-address holder, or interacting with a Company smart contract does not by itself confer actual, apparent, or general authority to bind the Company. A company-side act linked to a Protocol Governance decision requires both a Company Governance Approval and Company records evidencing that the underlying Protocol decision was validly taken and, where required, executed under the then-effective Protocol Governance. The Company Governance Approval extends only to the company-side act and is not an approval, re-approval, or redefinition of the underlying Protocol decision. Before signing or executing, each signer or agent shall confirm that the action matches the applicable Company Governance Approval and, where applicable, the foregoing Protocol Governance records. No signer or agent may initiate a Company execution transaction on its own initiative.

## ARTICLE VI: ACCOUNTING AND RECORDS

VI.1 Required Records. The following records shall be kept and maintained at the Principal Office (Article I.4), in electronic or access-controlled repositories selected by the Company from time to time, or, where appropriate for public records only, in Official Online Venues or smart contracts referenced in this Agreement, for a minimum of five (5) years, even where the Company has been dissolved or has otherwise ceased to exist, and in a manner that permits them to be made available for lawful inspection within a reasonable time:

- (a) A complete, reliable, and up-to-date list of current and former Members and/or the unique Distributed Ledger identifier(s) attributable to each Member;
- (b) A copy of the Certificate of Formation and all amendments thereto, together with executed copies of any powers of attorney pursuant to which the Certificate of Formation has been executed;
- (c) A copy of this Agreement, including all amendments thereto;
- (d) Any meeting minutes, written or electronic consents or resolutions, accounting records, financial statements, and other records reasonably necessary to evidence material actions and decisions of the Company and the authority for them;
- (e) As long as actions, transactions, voting, and decisions of the Company take place on a distributed ledger and where human-readable explanations of those actions, transactions, voting, and decisions are publicly available for a period of five (5) years after the date the Company is dissolved, in accordance with the DAO Act, there shall be no separate requirement to keep books of accounts and meeting minutes. In accordance with §711(4) of the DAO Act, except for relevant government officials with reasonable grounds to investigate a violation of the laws of the Republic, no one shall have the right under the LLC Act to demand to separately inspect or copy records of the Company, and the Company shall have no obligation to furnish any information to anyone concerning its activities, financial condition, or other circumstances to the extent the information is publicly available on a distributed ledger.

## ARTICLE VII: ASSETS AND PAYMENTS

VII.1 Foundation Operating Assets. "Foundation Operating Assets" means funds or other assets actually received and accepted by the Company for its own non-profit operations in accordance with this Agreement, Company Governance, and applicable law. Foundation Operating Assets include (i) donations, grants, gifts, bequests, and other contributions accepted for that purpose and (ii) assets received and accepted for that purpose pursuant to a company-side act covered by a Company Governance Approval and, where that act relates to a Protocol Governance decision, supported by the evidence required under Article V.4. Clause (ii) includes assets received through the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet after those requirements have been satisfied. Receipt in that Wallet or any other Company-administered account or wallet does not by itself cause an asset to qualify as a Foundation Operating Asset. The Company may manage and use Foundation Operating Assets only in connection with Foundation affairs (Article V.2), to further the Company's purposes, and within the scope of a Company Governance Approval, in each case in accordance with this Agreement and applicable law. Foundation Operating Assets exclude the Protocol Treasury, user assets, and assets that the Company merely administers or references in connection with Protocol Governance. The Company shall not accept any asset where prohibited by applicable law or its then-current compliance procedures. The Company shall maintain the records required by Article VI and the separation required by Article VII.3. Asset-level details, account or wallet information, transaction records, approvals, accounting or tax treatment, and supporting evidence are maintained in the Company's non-public records and do not form part of this Agreement.

VII.2 Protocol Treasury. The "Protocol Treasury" means Protocol revenue and other treasury assets administered pursuant to Protocol Governance. The Protocol Treasury is not property of the Company or an asset freely disposable by the Company, and the Company does not own or control it or have unilateral authority to direct its disposition. The Company may perform only the limited company-side administrative acts permitted by Articles II.2 and V.4 in relation to the Protocol Treasury. Any such administrative involvement does not cause the Protocol Treasury to become a Foundation Operating Asset. Any movement or spending of the Protocol Treasury is a Protocol Governance matter and is not executed by the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet or any signer or agent of the Company as an authority of the Company.

VII.3 Separation of Assets. The Company shall maintain the Foundation Operating Assets separately from the Protocol Treasury, user assets, and the assets of any Member or its holder. The Company shall not characterize the Protocol Treasury or user assets as Foundation Operating Assets or commingle them with Foundation Operating Assets. The applicable records, accounts or wallets, and approval processes shall reflect and preserve that separation.

VII.4 Services, Grants, and Reimbursements. Solely from Foundation Operating Assets, the Company may: (a) pay reasonable compensation, on arm's-length terms, for services actually rendered under a written agreement; (b) reimburse actual and reasonable expenses incurred on behalf of the Company that were approved in advance or, in the case of reasonable pre-formation expenses, ratified by Company Governance after formation; and (c) make grants under written grant agreements that directly further the Company's non-profit purposes. No such arrangement or payment may constitute an ownership distribution, profit share, residual claim, guaranteed take, circular pass-through, or compensation for services not rendered. Any related-party arrangement shall be subject to disclosure, recusal under Articles III.5 and III.7, and approval through Company Governance without the participation of the conflicted Member. The Company shall retain records reasonably sufficient to support each arrangement, approval, and payment under Article VI.

VII.5 No Contractual Control over Company Assets. No person, whether alone or together with affiliates or others, may hold any right under a contract, side letter, or other arrangement to determine substantially all policies governing the disposition of Foundation Operating Assets, and the Company shall not enter into or recognize any such arrangement.

This Article does not limit ordinary voting by Members or recusal with respect to related-party matters.

## ARTICLE VIII: DISSOCIATION OF A MEMBER

VIII.1 Member Status Cessation. A Member shall cease to be a Member automatically (such cessation, a "Dissociation", and any event described below, a "Dissociation Event"; "Dissociate" and "Dissociated" shall be construed accordingly) if:

- (i) the Member ceases to hold any Membership Interest as described in Article III;
- (ii) its holder is or becomes a Restricted Person under Article IX;
- (iii) the Member is the subject of a determination deemed a Dissociation Event under Article XII;
- (iv) its holder ceases to exist or permanently loses control of the Member address without an eligible successor approved under Articles III and IX; or
- (v) its holder breaches the concentration cap in Article III.7, and the breach is not remedied under the Company's then-current procedures.

A Member, acting through its holder, may also resign or withdraw by notice to the Company under Article XIV.5, effective on the date stated in the notice or, if no date is stated, when the notice is received.

VIII.2 Aftermath of Dissociation. If the number of Members falls below five, each remaining Member's Membership Interest remains twenty percent (20%), and the vacancy is not reallocated. While the vacancy continues, no voluntary dissolution under Article X.1(a) or amendment affecting a Protected Provision under Article XI.3 may be approved, and the Company shall admit a replacement under Article IX without undue delay. Dissociation does not by itself change the signer set or any signer authority of the Foundation Operating Multisig Wallet. Any separate change to a signer designation is governed by Article V.2 and Company Governance. The Company shall make or update any filing required by applicable law and update its records to reflect the change in Membership.

VIII.3 No Distribution on Cessation of Member Status. The voluntary resignation or withdrawal or involuntary removal or withdrawal of a person's Member status does not give rise to any right of a Member to a distribution of any property or economic resources that may be or become available to the Company.

## ARTICLE X: DISSOLUTION

X.1 Dissolution Events. The Company shall be dissolved and its affairs wound up upon: (a) approval by at least four of the five Members (4-of-5), through Company Governance, of a proposal submitted to all Members specifically for that purpose; or (b) any event other than a Member vote that requires dissolution under applicable law.

X.2 Dissolution Requirements. Following dissolution, the Company shall make the filings and take the actions required by applicable law to wind up and terminate the Company.

X.3 No Distribution to Members on Dissolution. The voluntary or involuntary dissolution of the Company does not give rise to any right of a Member to a distribution of any property or economic resources that may be or become available to the Company.

X.4 No Distribution to Interested Parties on Dissolution. Upon a court order dissolving the Company following a successful petition from an interested party, that interested party shall not have any right to a distribution of any property or economic resources that may be or become available to the Company.

X.5 Distribution of Residual Assets to a Non-Profit. Upon dissolution and after settling all debts and liabilities, any remaining property or economic resources of the Company shall be transferred to one or more non-profit or charitable entities organized for the same purposes as the Company, selected through Company Governance or otherwise in accordance with applicable law. No residual assets shall be distributed to any Interested Person.

## ARTICLE XII: DISPUTE RESOLUTION

XII.1 Dispute Resolution Process. Unless a different process is set out in other governance rules, in the event of a conflict or dispute between the parties under this Agreement, the following default dispute resolution process applies:

- (a) The parties to the conflict shall negotiate in good faith to resolve the matter.
- (b) A proposal shall be submitted to all Members through Company Governance in a form that redacts or otherwise protects, as appropriate, confidential information, personal information, legally privileged information, and information whose disclosure is restricted by law. The proposal shall describe:
  - (i) the nature of the dispute;
  - (ii) the amount or range of any compensation or damages claimed;
  - (iii) the fees quoted by an independent person or authority to be appointed to arbitrate;
  - (iv) whether funds are requested from the Company; and
  - (v) the reasons good-faith negotiations have not resolved the matter.
- (c) If the proposal is approved in accordance with Article III.5 (through Article V.1), the parties must act in accordance with the proposal.
- (d) If the dispute is not satisfactorily resolved, it shall be finally settled pursuant to Article XII.2 below.

XII.2 Alternative Dispute Resolution.

- (a) Should a controversy, dispute, or claim arise between or among any of the parties to this Agreement (including the Company, any Member, and any holder) out of or in relation to this Agreement ("Dispute"), the party raising the Dispute must give thirty (30) days' notice of such Dispute to each other party to the Dispute and, if the Company is not itself a party to the Dispute, to the Company (the "Notice of Dispute").
- (b) Should the Dispute remain at the expiration of thirty (30) days after service of the Notice of Dispute, the Dispute shall be settled by arbitration administered by the International Centre for Dispute Resolution in accordance with its International Arbitration Rules (the "Rules"). The arbitration shall be seated in the Republic of the Marshall Islands and governed by the laws of the Republic of the Marshall Islands. The language of the arbitration shall be English. The arbitration shall be determined by a sole arbitrator appointed in accordance with the Rules. Any award shall be in writing and final and binding on the parties without any right of appeal, and judgment upon any award may be entered in or enforced by any court having jurisdiction thereof.
- (c) Nothing in this Article prevents a party from seeking interim or conservatory measures from a court of competent jurisdiction or from seeking recognition or enforcement of an arbitral award.
- (d) In the event that it is determined pursuant to this Article XII that a Member who is party to a Dispute (i) is in material breach of this Agreement, (ii) acts in material violation of any statutory requirements of the Republic of the Marshall Islands or the laws or regulations of any other applicable jurisdiction, or (iii) acts against the best interests of the Company, such determination shall be deemed a Dissociation Event, as defined in Article VIII, for such Member.
- (e) No Member, holder, signer, or agent of the Company shall be required, by arbitration, emergency relief, or otherwise, to deliver or disclose any private or cryptographic signing key. Any lawful transfer of signing authority or compliance with interim or final relief shall, where reasonably practicable, be effected through signer rotation, reconstitution of the multi-signature arrangement, or a transaction authorized through Company Governance, rather than by delivery of a private key. Access mechanisms belonging to the Company (such as shared credentials) may be delivered pursuant to an authorization adopted through Company Governance, with the consent of the person concerned, in compliance with a binding order or award of an arbitrator or arbitral tribunal, or in compliance with a binding order or judgment of a court of competent jurisdiction.

## ARTICLE XIII: PROTOCOL CLAIMS, LIABILITY BACKSTOP, AND INDEMNIFICATION

XIII.1 Protocol Claims; Legal and Administrative Interface. For purposes of this Agreement, a "Protocol Claim" means any actual or threatened claim, demand, action, suit, investigation, inquiry, enforcement proceeding, arbitration, or other judicial, administrative, regulatory, or dispute-resolution proceeding arising, or alleged to arise, out of or relating to the Protocol, including its smart contracts, Protocol Governance, asset pools and mechanisms, interfaces, intellectual property, documentation, or ecosystem activities. To the extent permitted by applicable law, applicable procedural rules, and this Agreement, the Company may act as a legal and administrative interface and, subject to this Article, a liability backstop in respect of a Protocol Claim. Without thereby admitting or assuming liability, the Company may receive notices and service of process directed to the Company; retain counsel and other professional advisers; investigate, respond to, appear in or participate in, defend, negotiate a proposed resolution of, and otherwise administer a Protocol Claim in its own name; and, with the express written authorization of an Indemnified Person, support the defense of that person. No action under this Article, without more, creates a duty or payment right in favor of any claimant or other third party.

XIII.2 Claim-Specific Financial Commitments. The Company may voluntarily assume, settle, fund, indemnify against, or otherwise undertake financial liability arising from a Protocol Claim (a "Claim Commitment") only pursuant to a Company Governance Approval that expressly addresses the particular Protocol Claim. An approval may cover identified factually related claims arising from the same events, but shall not establish a standing category of claims for which financial liability is assumed. The approval shall identify the Protocol Claim and proposed beneficiary or counterparty, the nature and maximum aggregate amount of the Claim Commitment, the intended source of payment, any insurance expected to apply, and the Members' good-faith determination, subject to all applicable disclosure and recusal requirements, that the Claim Commitment:

- (a) furthers and is consistent with the non-profit purposes of the Company;
- (b) will not render the Company insolvent, unable to carry out its purposes, or unable to meet its liabilities from the fair value of its remaining assets;
- (c) does not constitute a guarantee of the issuance, redemption, repurchase, price, value, or performance of JPYdf; and
- (d) does not require or authorize the use of the Protocol Treasury or any user assets.

Before approving a Claim Commitment, the Members shall consider the relevant conduct and authorization, available defenses, insurance, expected costs, and source of funds. A conflicted person shall not control the Company's choice of counsel, response, settlement, or payment. Any settlement, indemnity, assumption, or other agreement with another person shall be in writing, state the maximum amount and permitted sources of payment, and be expressly covered by the applicable Company Governance Approval.

XIII.3 Indemnification of Indemnified Persons. "Indemnified Persons" means the Members and their holders, authorized signers, representatives, agents, contributors, service providers, delegates, and any other person expressly authorized in writing to act for or provide services to the Company, in each case only while acting within that written Company role or authorization. Subject to applicable law and this Agreement, the Company may indemnify, defend, and hold harmless an Indemnified Person against claims, liabilities, damages, judgments, settlements, costs, and reasonable attorneys' fees arising from that person's good-faith, authorized service to the Company. Status as an Indemnified Person, or participation in any activity of or for the Company or in any Protocol activity, does not, by itself, create an entitlement to indemnification. No indemnification shall be provided to the extent the Company determines in good faith on the basis of the available record, subject to applicable recusal requirements, or a final, non-appealable judgment or award establishes, that the relevant loss arose from that person's fraud, gross negligence, willful misconduct, bad faith, or material violation of this Agreement or applicable sanctions or law.

The Company may advance reasonable defense costs only pursuant to a separate Company Governance Approval and a written undertaking by the recipient to repay any amount ultimately determined not to be indemnifiable.

XIII.4 Sources of Payment; Insurance, Solvency, and Records. Only Foundation Operating Assets and insurance proceeds actually available and collectible by the Company may be used to make a payment under this Article. Neither the Protocol Treasury nor any user assets may be used, pledged, encumbered, or purportedly made available for that purpose. Each Claim Commitment embodied in an agreement with another person shall, to the fullest extent permitted by applicable law, limit that person's recourse to the permitted sources and maximum amount stated in the agreement. No reference to insurance in this Agreement represents that insurance is in force or that any particular claim, person, loss, or cost is covered. No Claim Commitment may be undertaken, and no discretionary payment may be made, if the Company is then insolvent, would thereby be made insolvent or unable to carry out its purposes, or would be left with assets having a fair value insufficient to meet its liabilities. The Company may obtain and maintain insurance on terms and in amounts approved through Company Governance and shall retain records reasonably sufficient to support each material response, Claim Commitment, settlement, advancement, payment, and insurance position under Article VI.

XIII.5 No Admission, Guarantee, Insurance Business, or Protocol Control. The Company's receipt, administration, response, defense, negotiation, settlement, Claim Commitment, or indemnification of a Protocol Claim does not, solely by reason of that action:

- (a) constitute an admission that the Company is liable for the Protocol Claim;
- (b) make the Company the issuer, redemption obligor, guarantor, insurer, custodian, operator, owner, or controller of JPYdf or the Protocol;
- (c) cause the Protocol Treasury, any asset pool, or any user assets to become property of the Company;
- (d) confer on the Company any signing key, administrative role, governance authority, or control over the Protocol; or
- (e) expand the Company's authority beyond Articles II.2, II.6, V.2 to V.4, and VII.2 to VII.3.

No person acquires a payment right or other third-party-beneficiary right under this Article except under a separate written agreement expressly approved in accordance with Article XIII.2. Nothing in this Article authorizes the Company to issue any policy of insurance, accept any premium or other consideration for assuming a Protocol Claim, or carry on the business of assuming insurance risks.
